The arithmetic

Every output is traceable to one formula.

No hidden adjustment factors, no assumed dose, no recommended protocol. The calculator is a transparent unit-conversion surface.

Formulas used

  • mass (mg) ÷ diluent volume (mL)Concentration (mg/mL)
  • concentration (mg/mL) × 100Amount in 0.1 mL (mcg)
  • target amount (mcg) ÷ (concentration (mg/mL) × 1,000)Volume per aliquot (mL)
  • volume (mL) × 100U-100 syringe units
  • total mass (mcg) ÷ target amount (mcg)Aliquots per vial
The calculator assumes that the peptide dissolves completely and that the entered diluent volume is the final volume. Powder displacement, dead volume, filter holdup and syringe dead space are not modelled.

Worked example

5 mg peptide + 2 mL diluent + 250 mcg target amount

Concentration5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 mg/mL
Amount in 0.1 mL2.5 × 100 = 250 mcg
Volume per 250 mcg250 ÷ 2,500 = 0.1 mL
U-100 scale0.1 × 100 = 10 U
Aliquots per vial5,000 ÷ 250 = 20
Read it as a ratio, not a regimen. Change the target amount and the volume changes; the concentration stays fixed until the mass or diluent volume changes.

Before and after reconstitution

Plan the storage, not just the volume.

These are general laboratory handling prompts. The lot documentation and your institutional SOP take precedence.

Lyophilised powder

Typically stored frozen and desiccated, protected from light. Let the vial equilibrate before opening to reduce condensation.

Protect from light

Many peptides are light-sensitive. Use amber or foil-wrapped secondary containment where the protocol requires it.

Aliquot, don't repeatedly freeze

Repeated freeze–thaw can change aggregation and activity. Plan single-use aliquots where the material and protocol allow.

Use-by is lot-specific

Do not assume a universal beyond-use date. Ask for the lot data and follow the documented storage conditions.

Calculator FAQ

Questions about the arithmetic.

Is concentration the same as a dose?

No. This tool calculates concentration and aliquot volume from the numbers you enter. It does not recommend an amount, frequency or route, and nothing here is a dosing instruction. The target amount must come from your own approved laboratory protocol.

Why does the calculator show both mg/mL and mcg per 0.1 mL?

Milligram-per-millilitre is the standard concentration expression. Micrograms per 0.1 mL is a convenient small-volume check when a protocol is written around a 0.1 mL aliquot. Both are derived from the same concentration and are shown together to make unit conversion visible.

What if the calculated volume is larger than my syringe?

The U-100 row will flag volumes above 100 units. Use a larger syringe, split the measurement, or revisit the target amount and diluent volume with your protocol. Do not estimate past the graduations of the syringe you have.

Does the calculator account for dead volume or powder displacement?

No. It assumes complete dissolution and that the diluent volume is the final volume. Real vials have dead volume, needle holdup and sometimes powder displacement. Those losses are separate from the arithmetic and should be characterised in your own validation.

Can I share a calculation with the supplier?

Yes. The WhatsApp, email and copy buttons include the mass, diluent volume, target amount, calculated concentration, aliquot volume and a link to the same calculation. Add your destination and quantity before sending.
