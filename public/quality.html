METHOD / LOT FILES

How a record is checked.

The useful question is not whether a website displays a seal. It is whether the lot in the box has an identity record, a stated analytical method and a net content that can be checked against the order.

THE LOT FILE

What a useful lot file should tell you.

Exact CoA layouts vary by producer and material. At minimum, the document should let you connect the analytical result to the lot you are buying.

Product and lot identity

Product name, register identifier, lot or batch number, fill or net content, and the date of analysis. The lot number should match the vial label and packing list.

Identity method and result

A stated method (for example HPLC retention, mass spectrometry or peptide mapping) and a result that supports the claimed identity. A “passed” tick without a method is difficult to audit.

Purity method and result

The chromatographic method, wavelength and integration basis, plus the reported purity. Ask whether related substances or counter-ions are included in the method.

Appearance and net content

Description of the lyophilised cake or solution, fill verification and any visible particulates. This is where a label claim and the physical vial meet.

Storage and handling

Recommended storage for the lot, any special precautions, and the basis for the stated retest or beyond-use period after reconstitution.

Scope and limitations

What the CoA does not cover: sterility, endotoxin, residual solvents or elemental impurities may require separate methods. Do not assume tests that are not reported.

Review, don't assume. A CoA is a lot-specific record. Ask for the document for the lot you will receive and compare it with the order. We do not present a generic certificate as proof of a specific lot.

HOW THE REGISTER IS BUILT

Identity data, with the gaps left visible.

The source product list contains names, pack schedules and pricing. It does not contain CAS numbers or formulas. Those fields are added only where a reliable public structure record exists.

Step 1 — Normalize the register

Multiple labels for the same material are aggregated into one record, so a “BPC 157 10 mg” listing and a “BPC-157 5 mg” listing do not become two competing pages. Each pack schedule remains visible in the pack table.

Step 2 — Add public chemistry only on a confident match

Where a product has a public structure record, the page shows the CAS, formula, molecular weight and a link to the PubChem CID. Where the name is a blend, protein, proprietary mixture or supplier code, the page says that a single identity does not apply rather than filling the field with a guess.

Step 3 — Keep the lot as the authority

Public chemistry describes an ideal molecule. The actual vial is governed by the sequence, salt form, fill and analytical results in the lot CoA. A public CAS is a starting point, not a release specification.

Step 4 — Show the evidence boundary

Each entry carries an evidence label: established target, clinical-stage, preclinical, exploratory, cosmetic ingredient, supplier blend, biologic or laboratory supply. The label describes the maturity of the public research context, not the quality of a particular lot.

CLAIMS AND LIMITS

What we do not put on a page.

A clean design can create false confidence. These are the claims we deliberately avoid unless the underlying document supports them.

Unheld certifications

No ISO, GMP, FDA or third-party seal is displayed unless the specific facility or lot holds it and the claim can be documented.

Therapeutic claims

No human or veterinary dosing, treatment claims or clinical promises. The register is for laboratory research only.

Invented identity

No plausible-looking CAS or formula for an ambiguous name. If the source list does not establish identity, the entry says so.

Pricing transparency. List prices are per pack and shown with the format. Freight, duties, taxes and bank charges are excluded. A formal quotation confirms the exact fill, availability and document set. See the full disclaimer.

LOT-FILE QUESTIONS

What buyers ask before releasing a lot.

Do you provide a certificate of analysis for every lot?

Ask for the lot-specific CoA for the material you are considering. It should identify the product, lot, identity method, purity method and result, net content, appearance and date. Availability of a specific document is confirmed on quotation; we do not treat a generic certificate as lot data.

Are the CAS numbers on the site independently verified?

CAS and formula values are added only where a confident public structure record exists, and the page links to the PubChem record where applicable. If the source catalog does not provide a reliable identity, the page says so. The lot CoA remains the governing identity document for shipped material.

Do you display ISO, GMP or third-party certification marks?

We do not display certification marks we cannot substantiate for the specific material or facility. If a document is relevant to a quotation, ask for it directly and we will state what is available rather than implying a certification through a website badge.

Why do some prices differ from other websites?

Prices reflect the pack format, product family, documentation and commercial terms quoted here. A different website may be quoting a different fill, salt form, purity specification, pack count or freight basis. Compare the CoA and the exact pack, not the headline number.
